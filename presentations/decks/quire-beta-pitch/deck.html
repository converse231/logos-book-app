Why tracking apps don't stick

They log the finish. Nobody finishes.

What gets tracked today
  • A book, once, when it's done
  • A shelf that mostly reminds you what you abandoned
  • Nothing at all for the two weeks in between
What actually needs tracking
  • The twenty minutes you read tonight
  • The day you didn't skip
  • The pace you're quietly holding

Land the left column first — it's the familiar, slightly sad experience. Then the right.

The reward has to land on the session, not the finish line. A 400-page book is three weeks of no feedback whatsoever.

The north star
Treat readers like athletes

Streaks, personal bests, levels you earn a name for. The gamification isn't bolted onto the tracker — it is the product.

This artwork is real in-app streak art — it's what you see when your streak is running.

The line that matters: a runner doesn't log the marathon, they log every run. Readers deserve the same instrument.

The core loop

Four things that pull you back

Streaks
Consecutive reading days, with a grace window so one bad night doesn't erase a month.
Levels with names
XP on every session. You don't reach level 3 — you become a Chapter Chaser.
Comeback Challenge
Break a streak and you get a short challenge to rebuild it, instead of a zero and a shrug.
Reading Insights
Occasional, unpredictable notes about your own reading — a milestone, a best-ever session, the pace you're on.

Name all four, then expand on one — Comeback Challenge is the one people react to, because every other tracker punishes the miss.

Insights are deliberately variable. Not every session gets one. That's the point.

A timer that gets out of the way

The contrast is the argument. While you're reading: a title, a clock, a pause button. Nothing to tap, nothing to look at.

The instant you stop, everything arrives at once — pages, minutes, XP, the fox. All the reward is at the end so none of it interrupts the reading.
